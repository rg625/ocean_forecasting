Since the gap is a reconstruction floor, I tested the two most direct remedies on the selected
configuration: a larger latent and a heavier reconstruction weight. These are four short runs
(6k steps, one seed each), compared on validation only.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
Run & Latent size & Best val.\ error (60 steps) & First-10-step error (end) & Energy kept (60)\\
\midrule
R0 reference              & 256  & 4.48 & 0.58 & 0.97\\
R1 larger latent          & 512  & 4.12 & 0.56 & 0.99\\
R2 much larger latent     & 1024 & 4.50 & \textbf{0.49} & 0.97\\
R3 reconstruction $\times5$ & 256 & 4.40 & 0.53 & 0.95\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_repr.pdf}
\caption{Representation tests (single seed). Neither a 4$\times$ larger latent nor a 5$\times$
reconstruction weight changes the 60-step error beyond noise.}
\label{fig:repr}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Result: neither remedy closes the gap.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Quadrupling the latent lowers the early-step error by only about 15\% (0.58 $\to$ 0.49).
  The overall 60-step error does not move beyond single-seed noise.
\item Weighting reconstruction 5$\times$ helps by a similar small amount.
\item The first-step gap to ACDM is 20--30$\times$, so latent size is not the bottleneck.
\item The likely causes are the encoder and decoder architecture themselves (they compress a
  $4\times64\times128$ field through one fully connected layer), and the joint training. The
  dynamics loss pulls the latent towards coordinates that are easy to propagate rather than
  easy to reconstruct.
\item The next things to test are a multiscale (U-Net-style) decoder and pre-training the
  autoencoder alone before adding the dynamics.
\end{itemize}
